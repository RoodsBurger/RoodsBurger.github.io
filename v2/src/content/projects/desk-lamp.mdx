## How it works

The desk lamp sets brightness by position: a brushless gimbal motor turns a lead screw, and a lit core with warm and cool LEDs rides up and down the body. Every brightness change is a mechanical move, so the motor, the light and the smart-home state all have to agree on where the core is.

The light fades in at the start of a rise and out at the end of a descent, so motion speed is also fade speed. At rest the motor driver sleeps, so there's no hum. I use it every day and sometimes get distracted just watching the core rise and fall.

## Controls

One knob runs the lamp. Turning it moves the core, a click toggles the light, and a double click switches the knob between motor, brightness and color temperature. Over Matter the lamp is a color temperature light, and its brightness slider drives the same motor position as the knob.

## Mechanism

A magnetic encoder closes the loop, so the firmware knows where the core is instead of counting steps and hoping they all landed. If someone stops the core by hand, the motor gives up after about half a second, takes that spot as the new target and reports the matching level over Matter.

The firmware is ESP-IDF with SimpleFOC, on an ESP32-S3 control board I designed. The field-oriented control loop gets one of the chip's two cores to itself, so radio traffic doesn't stall the motor.
