## What it is

The grinder is a hand grinder turned into a motorized appliance, run by a Raspberry Pi and a 1.28-inch round touchscreen. The hard part is that one Pi drives a stepper at a steady rate, feeds beans with a servo and runs a touch UI at once, in Python, with the display and the motor driver sharing one SPI bus.

I make espresso every day, and I built this because I got tired of grinding by hand. A stepper replaces the crank, an auger doser meters beans into the burr, and the Pi boots straight into the grinder interface as a systemd-managed appliance, with no keyboard, mouse or login. I modeled the body and hopper in Fusion 360 and printed them.

## Grinding and dosing

Two screens, switched with a horizontal swipe, set the grind speed (30 to 150 RPM at the burr) and the bean feed rate as a percentage. A center button starts and stops everything. Press it and the burr spins up, and 3 seconds later the doser starts feeding, so beans never land on a burr that isn't up to speed yet. The dial locks while grinding so a stray touch can't change the speed.

The doser is a continuous-rotation servo turning a bean auger. Cheap continuous servos stall at low speed, so it runs at full torque in short fixed bursts, and the feed rate comes from the spacing between bursts.

## Why three processes instead of threads?

The software runs as a UI process, a motor process and a servo process, for three reasons:

1. Timing. The motor process generates the stepper pulse train with busy-wait timing, and running it as its own process keeps it clear of Python's global interpreter lock.
2. The shared bus. The display and the DRV8711 sit on the same SPI bus, which two processes can't hold at once. The UI closes its SPI handle before starting the motor process. The motor process opens the bus only to write driver registers, closes it within milliseconds, then steps the motor over plain GPIO. When the grind stops, the UI reopens the bus.
3. Crashes. If either child process dies, the UI shuts both down cleanly and resets the screen.

## Touch and display

The screen is drawn with Pillow at twice the resolution and downscaled, so arcs and text come out anti-aliased on the small round panel. The start and stop icons, whole beans and ground coffee, are generated procedurally with a fixed random seed so they look the same on every boot. The touch controller goes silent while a finger is held still, so a small state machine infers release by timeout, and a swipe always overrides a pending tap.

## Hardware

A NEMA 23 stepper drives the burr through a 2:1 gearbox. It runs off a TI DRV8711 on a driver board I designed, which also carries headers for the Pi, the doser servo and the display. The display is a 240×240 round GC9A01 panel over SPI with a CST816T capacitive touch controller over I2C.

Getting the drive quiet took a while. I swept about 90 combinations of current, decay mode, blanking and microstepping, rated the noise of each, and settled on the quiet profile it runs now.
