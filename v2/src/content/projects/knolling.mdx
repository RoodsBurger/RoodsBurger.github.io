## What it is

TidyNET is a system that takes a photo of a cluttered workspace, generates an image of the same objects arranged neatly, and has a robot arm rearrange the real table to match. The plan is a picture, so the generated image has to keep every object recognizable, and the robot has to work out which object goes where and move each one without collisions.

I built it in 2024 and 2025 as a graduate research assistant in Columbia University's Creative Machines Lab. It extends the lab's knolling work, which studies how robots can learn to arrange objects neatly. I'm not an author of the lab's knolling papers; TidyNET is my own system built on top of that line of work.

## Planning in image space

The planner is a conditional denoising diffusion model (DDPM) I wrote by hand: a U-Net encoder and decoder with skip connections, group normalization, time embeddings at several levels, self-attention, and conditioning on the messy input image. It uses a cosine noise schedule and classifier-free guidance, and trains on paired before-and-after images at 256×256 pixels. It keeps each object's identity as it moves things into a tidy layout, though busier scenes still come out with occasional artifacts. The first experiments, in December 2024, used an off-the-shelf DDPM at 128 pixels and briefly tried ControlNet before I moved to my own implementation.

## Reading the scene

A YOLO model with oriented bounding boxes finds the objects, so each detection carries the object's rotation as well as its position. For training labels, I bootstrapped detections with a pretrained Faster R-CNN, matched them to known object positions with the Hungarian algorithm, assigned orientations, and then trained the oriented-box detector as a multi-task model over two datasets. The same detector runs on the camera photo and on the generated tidy image, and objects are matched between the two by color, again with the Hungarian algorithm.

## Executing on the arm

The arm is an Interbotix WidowX 200 with an Intel RealSense camera, under ROS2. Detections are projected into 3D with the camera intrinsics, tracked in a workspace manager and assigned to target slots. A grid occupancy map at 2 cm resolution, plus Separating Axis Theorem checks on oriented rectangles, catches collisions between objects. When a target spot is blocked by another object, the planner moves the blocker first, to a temporary spot if it needs one, then places the rest in priority order and checks the final positions.

## Results

On the real arm, TidyNET reaches 74.2% placement success and completes 70% of end-to-end tidying tasks; the other 30% don't finish. The training dataset belongs to the lab and stays private under its data agreement. In April 2025 I also contributed the control, detection and arm calibration code to the lab's Knolling repository.
