## What it is

TidyNET takes a photo of a cluttered workspace, generates an image of the same objects arranged neatly, and has a robot arm rearrange the real table to match. Because the plan is a picture, the generated image has to keep every object recognizable, and the robot has to work out which object goes where.

I built it in 2024 and 2025 as a graduate research assistant in Columbia's Creative Machines Lab, on top of the lab's knolling work. I'm not an author of the lab's knolling papers.

## Planning in image space

The planner is a conditional diffusion model (DDPM) I implemented myself: a U-Net with self-attention and time embeddings, conditioned on the messy image, with a cosine noise schedule and classifier-free guidance, trained on before-and-after pairs at 256×256. It keeps each object's identity, though busier scenes still come out with occasional artifacts.

## From images to the arm

A YOLO detector with oriented bounding boxes reads both the camera photo and the generated image, so each detection carries a rotation as well as a position. Objects are matched between the two by color with the Hungarian algorithm. The arm is a WidowX 200 under ROS2. A 2 cm occupancy grid and Separating Axis Theorem checks catch collisions, and the planner first moves any object sitting on another's target spot.

## Results

On the real arm, TidyNET reaches 74.2% placement success and finishes 70% of end-to-end tidying tasks. The training dataset belongs to the lab and stays private. I also contributed the control, detection and calibration code to the lab's Knolling repository.
