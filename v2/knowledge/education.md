## M.S. in Computer Science, Columbia University (2025)

Rodolfo Raimundo earned an M.S. in Computer Science from Columbia University's School of Engineering and Applied Science, attending from August 2024 and graduating in May 2025. His coursework focused on machine learning and robotics: Computer Vision I and II, Reinforcement Learning, Robotics Studio (a hardware course in Mechanical Engineering), Natural Language Processing, a project-based advanced deep learning course, and Deep Learning for Robot Manipulation. Rodolfo also completed two terms of supervised research in Columbia's Creative Machines Lab, which produced TidyNET. The reinforcement learning and robotics coursework connects to his quadruped project, Tobias.

## B.A. in Computer Science, Columbia University (2021)

Rodolfo received a B.A. in Computer Science with a concentration in Mathematics from Columbia College, Columbia University, attending from 2017 and graduating in May 2021. The mathematics side included calculus, linear algebra, analysis and optimization, a full year of modern algebra, and real analysis. His computer science courses included machine learning, analysis of algorithms, advanced algorithms, cryptography, computational complexity, computer systems, and Programming and Problem Solving, a course built around team strategy tournaments in Java. As an undergraduate Rodolfo also took Computation and the Brain, a graduate seminar, which produced his Artificial Synaptic Pruning project in Fall 2020.

## Technical degree in Electronics, IFSP (2016)

Before Columbia, Rodolfo completed a high school technical degree in Electronics at the Instituto Federal de Educação, Ciência e Tecnologia de São Paulo (IFSP) in Brazil, class of 2016. In 2015 he did research on the Chua-Matsumoto chaotic circuit, a classic physical circuit that produces chaotic behavior, advised by a faculty member at IFSP and funded by CNPq, Brazil's national research council. Rodolfo wrote a Fortran program to compute Lyapunov exponents and a MATLAB simulation to compare against measurements, and presented the findings at a symposium of Brazil's federal institutes. His final project was an FFT audio spectrum analyzer written in C, with circuitry designed in Proteus and a 3D-printed case. This electronics training is the root of his later firmware and circuit board work.
