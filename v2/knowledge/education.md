## M.S. in Computer Science, Columbia University (2025)

Rodolfo Raimundo earned an M.S. in Computer Science from Columbia University's School of Engineering and Applied Science, attending from August 2024 and graduating in May 2025. His coursework focused on machine learning and robotics: Computer Vision I and II, Reinforcement Learning, Robotics Studio (a hardware course in Mechanical Engineering), Natural Language Processing, a project-based advanced deep learning course, and Deep Learning for Robot Manipulation. Rodolfo also completed two terms of supervised research in Columbia's Creative Machines Lab, which produced TidyNET. He graduated with a 4.036 GPA, earning A+ grades in three of his five final-semester courses, and received the department's Behring Foundation Award in 2025. The reinforcement learning and robotics coursework connects to his quadruped project, Tobias.

## B.A. in Computer Science, Columbia University (2021)

Rodolfo received a B.A. in Computer Science with a concentration in Mathematics from Columbia College, Columbia University, attending from 2017 and graduating in May 2021. The mathematics side included calculus, linear algebra, analysis and optimization, a full year of modern algebra, and real analysis. His computer science courses included machine learning, analysis of algorithms, advanced algorithms, cryptography, computational complexity, computer systems, and Programming and Problem Solving, a course built around team strategy tournaments in Java. As an undergraduate Rodolfo also took Computation and the Brain, a graduate seminar, which produced his Artificial Synaptic Pruning project in Fall 2020. Outside computer science, he took Introduction to East Asian Civilization: Korea in Spring 2021 and wrote two essays on Korean history for it. In Fall 2019 he took Introduction to Cryptography. He was a Science Research Fellow throughout his B.A. and made the Dean's List in Fall 2020.

## Stanford University, Non-Degree Option in Artificial Intelligence (2022)

Rodolfo Raimundo also studied at Stanford University. While working full time, he took graduate courses in artificial intelligence through Stanford's Non-Degree Option (NDO), which lets professionals enroll in regular Stanford graduate courses for credit without joining a degree program. He started in Winter 2022 with CS224N, Natural Language Processing with Deep Learning, and his Stanford coursework covered deep learning and robotics. Pindrop's education stipend paid for the courses. The Stanford classes renewed his interest in research and led to his decision to go back to school for the M.S. at Columbia in 2024.

## University of São Paulo (USP), before Columbia

Before moving to the United States, Rodolfo started university in Brazil at the Universidade de São Paulo (USP). He was enrolled in Computer Engineering at USP's Escola Politécnica and in Computer Science at its Institute of Mathematics and Statistics (IME-USP), and took Linear Algebra, Calculus, Programming in C, Physics, advanced Java programming, Materials Science and Chemistry there. He left USP without finishing a degree when he was admitted to Columbia, where he started his B.A. in 2017.

## Technical degree in Electronics, IFSP (2016)

Before Columbia, Rodolfo completed a high school technical degree in Electronics at the Instituto Federal de Educação, Ciência e Tecnologia de São Paulo (IFSP) in Brazil, class of 2016. In 2015 he did research on the Chua-Matsumoto chaotic circuit, a classic physical circuit that produces chaotic behavior, advised by a faculty member at IFSP and funded by CNPq, Brazil's national research council. Rodolfo wrote a Fortran program to compute Lyapunov exponents and a MATLAB simulation to compare against measurements, and presented the findings at a symposium of Brazil's federal institutes. His final project was an FFT audio spectrum analyzer written in C, with circuitry designed in Proteus and a 3D-printed case. This electronics training is the root of his later firmware and circuit board work.
