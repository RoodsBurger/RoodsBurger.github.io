## Korean history and society

Rodolfo Raimundo has studied Korean history and society. At Columbia he took Introduction to East Asian Civilization: Korea (ASCE UN1363) in Spring 2021, a survey of Korea from ancient times through the twentieth century, and he wrote two research essays for it. He is interested in the Three Kingdoms, the Koryŏ dynasty, and Korea's relations with Japan after the colonial era, and still reads and writes about Korean history.

## Essay: religion from the Three Kingdoms to early Koryŏ

Rodolfo's essay "A Socioreligious Journey: from the Three Kingdoms to Early Koryŏ" looks at how state religion shaped Korean society. It argues that in Goguryeo, Baekje and Silla, Buddhism was the official faith and gave rulers an ideology of unity and legitimacy: karma and rebirth justified aristocratic privilege and strict social rank, and groups like Silla's Hwarang tied young elites to the state. In Koryŏ, Confucianism became part of how the government was organized while Buddhism, still backed by the state, grew even more popular, with monk examinations for public office. The essay also covers the effects on Korea's relations with China, on Buddhist art, architecture and printing (including the invention of metal movable type), and on the economy, as tax-exempt monasteries grew rich enough to rival the state.

## Essay: Japanese colonial rule and Korea–Japan relations

Rodolfo's essay "The Diplomatic Actions and Reactions of the Japanese Colonial Rule" examines how Japan's colonization of Korea still shapes relations between the two countries. It covers forced conscription and the "comfort women" system, the 1965 normalization treaty under Park Chung-hee, the 2015 compensation agreement and its later rejection under Moon Jae-in, the 2018 forced-labor court rulings against Japanese companies, Japan's 2019 export controls on chip materials, the BoycottJapan consumer campaign, and South Korea's decision not to renew the GSOMIA intelligence-sharing agreement. It concludes that colonial rule and World War II shaped not only Korea's economy and politics but the diplomatic dynamics of the whole region.

## Retroactive Soccer: a strategy tournament report

In Fall 2020, in Columbia's Programming and Problem Solving course (COMS W4444, taught by Professor Kenneth Ross), Rodolfo and two teammates built a player for Retroactive Soccer, a game where each team can reallocate its goals after every round and teams are ranked by average rank across rounds. Their group tried decision trees, rank-difference sorting, a constraint exploit, and linear and logistic regression to predict opponents, and mixed them into one final strategy. They placed third on average across the class tournament, and their report analyzes why, including constraint failures in the submitted code and fixes they would make.

## Mathematics and theory coursework

Rodolfo's undergraduate theory work included Introduction to Cryptography (COMS W4261) in Fall 2019, where his problem sets proved properties of collision-resistant hash functions, message authentication codes in the random oracle model, and one-way functions by reduction.
