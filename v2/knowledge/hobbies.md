## Climbing

Climbing is Rodolfo Raimundo's main hobby. He boulders up to V8 and sport climbs up to 5.12b, and when he can't get to a wall he's usually hanging off a fingerboard. Rodolfo is always up for a session. The training carried over into his software: he built RaiClimbing, a Flutter training tracker for climbing and general workouts that logs sessions set by set, imports his Tension Board climbs, scores pain check-ins for red flags, and runs an LLM analysis that suggests changes to his training plan, which he approves before anything changes.

## Ceramics

Rodolfo does ceramics, mainly wheel throwing. Pottery is his break from screens. He throws on the wheel, tries different clay bodies, and alters the shapes of pieces as he goes.

## Teaching and mentoring

Teaching is a long-running thread for Rodolfo. At about 16 he co-founded APOLE, a volunteer program in the state of São Paulo, Brazil, and from 2013 to 2019 it ran free math and physics classes for more than 1,000 public-school students. Rodolfo taught math and physics there himself. He also mentors Brazilian public-school students who are applying to university and pursuing higher education.

## Hiking and camping

Hiking and camping are how Rodolfo gets outdoors and away from screens and the workbench. He's working his way through as many US National Parks as he can, camping along the way, and he is planning his first multi-day trek, a climb up Mount Kilimanjaro. Along with climbing, ceramics and teaching, hiking is one of Rodolfo's four main personal interests outside of work.

## Coffee and everyday objects

Rodolfo makes espresso every day. He got tired of grinding by hand, so he motorized a hand grinder with a Raspberry Pi, a stepper motor on a driver board he designed, an auger bean doser and a round touchscreen. He likes designing objects he uses at home, including a rising-core desk lamp that he also uses every day and sometimes catches himself watching as the core goes up and down, and a kinetic wall lamp that moves when it's knocked.
