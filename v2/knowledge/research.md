## ACL 2026 paper on bias in AI text detectors

Rodolfo Raimundo is a co-author of "Identifying Bias in Machine-generated Text Detection," a peer-reviewed paper accepted to the main conference of ACL 2026, the Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, held in San Diego in July 2026. ACL is one of the top venues in natural language processing. The authors are Kevin Stowe, Svetlana Afanaseva, Rodolfo Raimundo, Yitao Sun and Kailash Patil, all at Pindrop. The paper is in the ACL Anthology (aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.109) and on arXiv (arxiv.org/abs/2512.09292).

## What the ACL paper found

The paper tests 16 systems that detect machine-generated text against about 41,700 student essays labeled by gender, race and ethnicity, English-language-learner status and economic status. The biases turned out to be specific to each detector rather than one shared failure. Essays by English-language learners were flagged as machine-generated more often across most detectors, and non-White English learners saw the worst false-positive rates. The more accurate detectors tended to be less biased. Human annotators were poor at telling human from machine text, at 45% to 53% accuracy, but showed no statistically significant demographic bias. The work was covered by Pindrop's blog and picked up by the trade press, and it has started to be cited by other researchers.

## Research track record

Research runs through Rodolfo's whole career. In high school he did CNPq-funded research on the Chua-Matsumoto chaotic circuit in Brazil and presented it at a symposium of Brazil's federal institutes and at the International Young Physicists' Tournament. At Columbia he was a Science Research Fellow, a Data Science Institute Scholar in Professor Eugene Wu's lab, and later a Graduate Research Assistant in Hod Lipson's Creative Machines Lab, where he built TidyNET. At Pindrop he is a Research Scientist, with an ACL 2026 paper and two bylined technical articles on the company's site, one on an agentic documentation system built on Claude and one on voice migration at the scale of millions of enrollments.
