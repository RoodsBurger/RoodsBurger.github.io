## Programming languages

Rodolfo Raimundo writes most of his machine learning, robotics, and Raspberry Pi code in Python. He uses C++ and C for embedded firmware on ESP32 microcontrollers, Swift with SwiftUI for native macOS apps, Dart with Flutter for cross-platform mobile and desktop apps, and TypeScript and JavaScript for web work, including this portfolio site. He has also used Java in coursework and at Morgan Stanley, SQL and PL/pgSQL for Postgres databases, Kotlin for an Android app, Fortran and MATLAB in early research, and LaTeX for documents. Rodolfo speaks Portuguese and English natively, and has elementary Spanish and French.

## Machine learning and deep learning

Rodolfo's machine learning work covers PyTorch, TensorFlow and Keras, scikit-learn, and MLflow for experiment tracking. He has built diffusion models from scratch, including a conditional U-Net DDPM with classifier-free guidance, and used Hugging Face diffusers. His audio work at Pindrop used transfer learning from pretrained networks and multi-task contrastive learning for authentication. He has trained YOLO detectors (v8 and v11, including oriented bounding boxes) and used Faster R-CNN to bootstrap labels. Rodolfo has also studied network pruning, and on the infrastructure side he has built training pipelines on AWS SageMaker.

## Robotics and reinforcement learning

In robotics, Rodolfo has trained walking policies with Soft Actor-Critic and DQN using PyBullet, Gymnasium, and Stable-Baselines3, and moved them to real hardware through trajectory replay and closed-loop IMU control. He has used ROS2 with an Interbotix WidowX 200 arm and an Intel RealSense camera, and worked with camera intrinsics, 2D-to-3D projection, arm calibration, Hungarian-algorithm matching, and collision checking with the Separating Axis Theorem. Rodolfo builds robot models from CAD, going from Fusion 360 to STL meshes to URDF files for simulation.

## Embedded systems and hardware

Rodolfo's hardware skills come from a technical degree in electronics and many personal builds. He writes firmware with ESP-IDF on ESP32-S3, C6, and C3 chips, runs field-oriented control of brushless motors with SimpleFOC, and tunes stepper drivers such as the DRV8711 and TMC2209. He integrates devices with smart-home platforms through Matter over WiFi, including per-device Matter credentials, and ships signed over-the-air firmware updates. Rodolfo designs custom circuit boards in EAGLE and Fusion Electronics, builds Raspberry Pi appliances managed by systemd, and has worked with SPI, I2C, UART, MQTT, and Bluetooth, as well as encoders, IMUs, capacitive touch controllers, and load cells.

## LLM and agent engineering

Rodolfo's current work at Pindrop puts LLM agents into research workflows: he built a documentation agent on Amazon Bedrock AgentCore that uses Claude and cut the time per model document from 25 days to 8. In his own apps he follows one pattern: code computes the numbers, the model judges what they mean, a server-side validator decides what reaches the user, and a person approves anything binding. He runs scheduled Claude Code routines that write structured analysis reports into his budgeting and training apps, and builds retrieval-augmented generation with Cohere embeddings, reranking and Command A over a Pinecone index, as in the chat on his site. Rodolfo also wrote an open-source Claude Agent Skill for circuit board design and review.

## Web, cloud, and apps

For web and cloud work, Rodolfo uses Astro, React, Tailwind CSS, and Netlify Functions, as on this site, and Node.js with Express. He builds app back ends on Supabase with Postgres, row-level security, realtime sync, edge functions, and scheduled jobs, and has integrated Plaid for bank data. On AWS he has used SageMaker, ECR, Docker, Batch, Bedrock AgentCore, and AWS CDK for infrastructure as code. Rodolfo builds native macOS menu bar apps with SwiftUI, OAuth, the Keychain, and FSEvents, and cross-platform apps with Flutter. For data and business intelligence he has used Tableau, Redash, and AWS QuickSight.

## Design and fabrication

Rodolfo designs the physical parts of his projects in Autodesk Fusion 360 and prints them on desktop FDM printers. He has modeled robot bodies, coffee grinder housings and hoppers, lamp mechanisms, and printed gearboxes, including planetary and cycloidal reductions. For the web, Rodolfo exports models from Fusion 360 through Blender to glTF so they can be shown as interactive 3D viewers on his portfolio, and he produces product renders in Blender.
