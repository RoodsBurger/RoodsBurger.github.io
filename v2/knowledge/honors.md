## GPA and grades

Rodolfo Raimundo finished his M.S. in Computer Science at Columbia University in May 2025 with a 4.036 cumulative GPA. Columbia counts an A+ as 4.33, which is how the GPA ends up above 4.0. His final semester was five courses, taken while he was building TidyNET, and he earned an A+ in three of them: Deep Learning for Robot Manipulation, the project-based advanced deep learning course, and his second term of supervised research in the Creative Machines Lab. His master's GPA is the grade figure he shares.

## Behring Foundation Award (Columbia, 2025)

In 2025 Rodolfo received the Behring Foundation Award from Columbia's Computer Science department, one of the department's named student awards for the class of 2025. The award goes to Computer Science students at Columbia Engineering who best show academic excellence, entrepreneurial interest and leadership, with preference for students who have lived, worked or studied in Brazil or elsewhere in Latin America. He is listed by name on the department's awards page.

## Undergraduate honors at Columbia

As an undergraduate at Columbia, Rodolfo was a Science Research Fellow, a four-year program whose fellows are nominated during admissions and get research funding and guaranteed research placements with Columbia scientists. He was a Data Science Institute Scholar in 2018, placed in Professor Eugene Wu's lab. He made the Dean's List in Fall 2020. In 2019 he was a Morgan Stanley Richard B. Fisher Scholar, a scholarship that came with his summer analyst internship at Morgan Stanley.

## Science olympiad medals in Brazil

In high school in Brazil, Rodolfo won four national gold medals in science olympiads: the Brazilian Physics Olympiad (OBF), the Public School Physics Olympiad (OBFEP), the Brazilian Robotics Olympiad (OBR) and the Brazilian Astronomy and Astronautics Olympiad (OBA). He also won a silver medal in the Public School Mathematics Olympiad (OBMEP) and an honorable mention at the International Young Physicists' Tournament (IYPT). His robotics team's Arduino and LEGO robot took first place regionally in 2015. At IFSP his FFT audio spectrum analyzer won Best Final Project, and he was a finalist in the BM&FBOVESPA stock investment challenge. In the same years he co-founded APOLE, which prepared public-school students for olympiads like these.
